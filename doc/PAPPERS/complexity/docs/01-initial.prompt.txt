# 1. THE CENTRAL CHANGE IN PERSPECTIVE

Do not frame the project primarily as:

> "We built a multi-agent AI trading system."

That is not the central scientific contribution.

HiFi should instead be treated as an **experimental platform for studying collective intelligence in AI-based financial decision making**.

The central research question we want to investigate is approximately:

> **When does a collection of heterogeneous AI agents produce better financial decisions than its individual members, and to what extent does this advantage depend on diversity and independence rather than merely model complexity or aggregation?**

This is the direction.

The exact wording of the research question may evolve after you inspect the repository and the evidence. Do not force the evidence to fit this wording.

The scientific contribution we ultimately want to investigate is the relationship between:

**agent diversity → information independence → decision correlation → collective performance**

and how this relationship changes across financial regimes and aggregation mechanisms.

The trading system is therefore primarily an **experimental laboratory**.

The AI models, RAG systems, GraphRAG, agents, aggregation mechanisms, verification system, temporal controls, and evaluation infrastructure are experimental components.

The financial market is the empirical environment.

The resulting paper should answer a scientifically meaningful question, not merely demonstrate that a particular implementation works.

# 3. THE EXISTING EVALUATION MUST BE TREATED AS A SCIENTIFIC WARNING

The repository contains an evaluation of the implementation against the HiFi "David".

The evaluation dated 2026-08-23 identifies a critical distinction:

* approximately 70% of the architectural system has been built;
* scientific trustworthiness of current empirical results was assessed at approximately 40%.

The evaluation explicitly identifies temporal leakage, survivorship bias, inconsistent price bases, weak verification, contaminated independence measurements, missing purged/embargoed validation, and other problems.

Treat those findings as **blocking scientific issues**, not as cosmetic engineering TODOs.

In particular, the following findings must be addressed before trusting headline empirical results:

### C1 — Point-in-time accuracy

Historical decisions must only have access to information genuinely available at the historical decision date.

This includes:

* vector retrieval
* GraphRAG
* SEC documents
* episodic memory
* agent memory
* macroeconomic data
* performance-weight calculations
* all other contextual information

Every retrieval path used in historical evaluation must obey an explicit `as_of` constraint.

A document's fiscal period is NOT equivalent to its availability date.

A label computed using future prices must never be available to a historical decision.

Macro data must respect publication/vintage timing.

Performance weights must not contain future performance.

---

### C2 — Survivorship bias

Historical evaluation must not use today's surviving universe as though it existed historically.

Implement or otherwise establish:

* historical universe membership
* additions
* deletions
* delistings
* terminal outcomes

Delisted companies must not disappear from the denominator simply because their future price series is incomplete.

A company that disappears must produce an appropriate terminal outcome rather than silently becoming missing data.

---

### C3 — Corporate-action consistency

Establish one coherent price-basis contract.

Do not allow adjusted and unadjusted prices to silently coexist in:

* signals
* indicators
* labels
* returns
* risk calculations
* covariance
* live data
* historical data

Corporate actions must not create artificial returns or indicator discontinuities.

---

### C4 — Verification validity

The current regex-based verification system can interpret zero extractable claims as zero hallucinations.

That is scientifically unacceptable.

The verification system must distinguish:

* zero hallucinations
* zero claims
* failed extraction
* unverifiable claims
* verified claims
* contradicted claims

Structured claims should become the primary mechanism, with regex fallback only where appropriate.

Claim coverage/density must itself be monitored.

---

### C5 — Fine-tuning contamination

Fine-tuned models must be evaluated against genuinely held-out data.

A model must not be trained on periods subsequently used to evaluate it.

Deployment must require demonstrable held-out performance.

Rejected adapters must never silently become production models.

Model identity and approval provenance must be recorded.

---

### C6 — Safety architecture

Although the paper is primarily scientific rather than operational, the research system must not contain misleading safety machinery.

The documented SafetyConfig must actually control the system.

Remove duplicate hard-coded limits.

Enforce quorum.

Make critical breakers fail closed.

Repair the running high-water mark logic.

Implement deterministic order idempotency and reconciliation where relevant.

Do not allow a system to claim a safety property merely because configuration values exist in a file.

---

### C7 — Agent independence

This is one of the most important scientific issues in the entire project.

The original design requires agents to reason independently before collective aggregation.

Shared information can make supposedly independent agents correlated.

Therefore distinguish carefully between:

**model diversity**

and

**information diversity / decision independence**.

Different models receiving identical information may not constitute meaningfully independent agents.

The research must explicitly investigate this.

Remove or isolate shared-context leakage before measuring diversity.

The debate mechanism must not reveal information that artificially induces conformity before independent reasoning has occurred.

---

### C8 — Evaluation protocol

Implement genuine walk-forward evaluation with:

* temporal ordering
* appropriate purge
* embargo
* horizon-aware separation
* no train/test contamination
* frozen evaluation sets

The system must explicitly test for leakage.

The fact that a walk-forward harness exists is not sufficient.

We need to demonstrate that the evaluation protocol itself is valid.

# 4. PRIMARY SCIENTIFIC HYPOTHESES

Begin from these hypotheses, but treat them as hypotheses to be tested rather than conclusions.

## H1 — Collective performance

A properly constructed ensemble of heterogeneous AI agents can outperform the average individual agent and potentially the strongest individual agent under identical information and evaluation conditions.

The comparison must be statistically defensible.

---

## H2 — Diversity and independence

The benefit of an AI ensemble is related to the degree of independence among its members.

We want to investigate whether lower decision correlation corresponds to greater ensemble improvement.

The key conceptual relationship is:

> **Diversity is valuable when it produces genuinely different decisions, not merely different model architectures.**

Measure this explicitly.

---

## H3 — Shared information reduces effective diversity

When supposedly independent agents receive substantially overlapping information, their decisions become more correlated and the marginal benefit of aggregation decreases.

This should be tested experimentally rather than asserted.

---

## H4 — Verification

A verification mechanism can improve the reliability of AI-generated financial reasoning, reducing unsupported or incorrect claims without simply suppressing useful information.

Measure this before and after verification.

---

## H5 — Regime dependence

The value of agent diversity and aggregation may vary according to market regime.

Investigate whether:

* bull/bear regimes
* high/low volatility
* trending/mean-reverting environments
* macro regimes

change the relationship between diversity and collective performance.

Do not assume the effect exists.

---

## H6 — Aggregation mechanism

Different aggregation mechanisms may perform differently depending on the degree of agent agreement, confidence calibration, diversity, and market regime.

Compare:

* majority voting
* confidence-weighted voting
* performance-weighted voting
* debate-based aggregation
* adaptive aggregation, if scientifically justified and properly trained

# 5. THE EXPERIMENTAL MATRIX

The final research programme should include, where justified and computationally feasible, experiments such as:

### A. Individual-agent baselines

Evaluate every agent independently:

* Fundamental
* Technical
* Risk
* Macro
* Sentiment
* Contrarian
* Valuation, if implemented

---

### B. Homogeneous ensembles

Same model family with meaningful variation.

This establishes whether "more agents" alone produces improvement.

---

### C. Heterogeneous ensembles

Different model families.

Test whether heterogeneity provides additional value.

---

### D. Information-independent heterogeneous ensemble

Different models receiving genuinely independent information channels where scientifically appropriate.

This is especially important.

---

### E. Shared-context condition

Deliberately provide overlapping/shared information.

This should function as an experimental condition for testing whether information correlation reduces collective benefit.

---

### F. Agent-removal ablations

Remove one agent at a time.

Measure:

* ensemble performance change
* diversity change
* correlation change
* regime-specific effects

---

### G. Model-homogenisation ablation

Replace heterogeneous models with a homogeneous model population.

Compare against the heterogeneous population.

---

### H. Memory ablation

Only conduct this once the ablation is verified to actually remove memory.

The previous project evaluation found an attempted memory ablation that effectively ablated nothing.

Do not repeat that mistake.

---

### I. Aggregation comparisons

Compare aggregation methods under the same underlying agent population.

---

### J. Classical baselines

At minimum investigate:

* random
* buy-and-hold where appropriate
* momentum
* equal-weight
* risk-budget strategy

Add other conventional financial baselines where justified.

---

### K. AI/model baselines

If scientifically appropriate and reproducible, compare against external/cloud models or other relevant AI baselines.

Do not add a baseline merely for marketing value.

Every baseline must answer a scientific question.
