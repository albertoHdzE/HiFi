11. Business Impact

This is one area where many technical interviewers fail.

A Senior Lead is not hired to build embeddings.

A Senior Lead is hired to create business value.

Ask:

Why was this project funded?
What KPI improved?
What ROI was achieved?
What was the baseline?
What would happen if the project disappeared tomorrow?

A surprising number of candidates can explain:

Pinecone
LangGraph
GPT-4

But cannot explain:

Why the company spent $500k on the project.

12. Evaluation Frameworks

This is huge.

Many candidates build systems.

Few know how to evaluate them.

Ask:

For RAG
How did you measure retrieval quality?
How did you measure answer quality?
How did you evaluate hallucinations?
Did you have golden datasets?
Did you perform human evaluation?
For Agents
How did you know the agents improved anything?
What metric justified multi-agent complexity?

This is one of my favorite filters.

13. Cost Awareness

The best senior engineers know money.

Ask:

What was your monthly OpenAI bill?
What was your inference cost?
How much did Pinecone cost?
Why didn't you self-host?

If someone built a large enterprise system and has absolutely no idea of costs, that tells you something.

14. Security and Compliance

Especially for enterprise roles.

Ask:

Did documents leave the company boundary?
How did you protect embeddings?

Many GenAI candidates completely collapse here.

15. Failure Stories

This is perhaps my favorite category.

Ask:

Tell me about a project that failed.

Then:

What did you learn?

Strong candidates often become MORE credible here.

Weak candidates often have:

"Everything was successful."

Which is suspicious.

20. Technical Taste

This is the hardest category.

The very best candidates have opinions.

Not dogma.

Opinions.

Ask:

What technology is overhyped today?
What is underrated?
What would you never use again?
What is your favorite vector database and why?

Experts accumulate preferences through pain.

People who only read blogs tend to answer:

"It depends."

for everything.

The Three Questions I Would Never Remove

After all the interviews we've gone through, if I could ask only three questions, they would be:

1. What was the hardest production problem you personally solved?

This reveals:

ownership,
depth,
scars,
seniority.
2. Why did you choose X instead of Y?

(Embeddings, chunking, vector DB, model, framework...)

This reveals:

tradeoff thinking.
3. What exactly did YOU do?

Not the team.

Not the project.

You.

This single question has exposed more exaggeration in our discussions than all the technology questions combined.

If I were building the "Alberto Interview Framework" from everything we've learned so far, I would score candidates in 12 dimensions:

Technical Depth
Technical Breadth
Production Experience
Ownership
Leadership
Business Impact
Evaluation & Metrics
Tradeoff Reasoning
Scaling Experience
Cost Awareness
Security & Compliance
Communication

A genuine Senior Data Science Lead should score at least 7/10 in every category, and ideally 8+ in Ownership, Production Experience, and Tradeoff Reasoning. Those three categories have been the strongest predictors of candidate quality across the interviews you've conducted.
